## 🚗 Cars & smart buying

**01 — Used-car inspection checklist**

> I am evaluating a [YEAR MAKE MODEL] with [MILEAGE] miles, asking price [PRICE], in [STATE/COUNTRY]. Create a prioritized pre-purchase inspection checklist organized into documents/history, engine, transmission, electrical systems, cooling, suspension, brakes, tires, safety recalls and road test. Give me 10 specific questions to ask the seller, symptoms that would justify walking away, and items a qualified independent mechanic must verify. Label every model-specific risk as UNVERIFIED until supported by an authoritative source. Do not claim you inspected this vehicle.

**02 — Compare two used cars**

> Compare [VEHICLE A, YEAR, MILEAGE, PRICE] and [VEHICLE B, YEAR, MILEAGE, PRICE] based on ownership cost, safety, practical needs [NEEDS], available inspection history, and repair risk. Distinguish verified facts from assumptions and mark unknown maintenance history as UNKNOWN. Recommend a buyer's next three verification steps.

**03 — Mechanic question translator**

> Rewrite these symptoms as a concise diagnostic description a technician can work with: [SYMPTOMS]. Include when they occur, driving conditions, dashboard indicators, prior repairs, and the difference between what was measured versus what was assumed. Do not guess a final diagnosis.

**04 — Car ownership cost**

> Build a 36-month cost-of-ownership estimate for [VEHICLE] driven [MILES/YEAR] in [LOCATION]. Separate known costs from unknowns. Include insurance, registration, depreciation, fuel/electricity, tires, maintenance, potential repairs and loan interest. Ask for missing essential inputs before calculating totals.

**05 — Service quote review**

> Review this repair estimate [PASTE, WITHOUT PERSONAL DATA]. Explain the likely labor operations, parts, warranty questions, additional procedures and what information is required to assess whether the estimate is reasonable. Don't assert current labor rates or fitment without sources.

## 💼 Work & productivity

**06 — Clear action plan**

> Turn the messy notes below into a one-page action plan with a goal, prioritized tasks, owner, deadline if known, and the first action to take today. Never invent dates or commitments. Notes: [NOTES]

**07 — Professional message**

> Rewrite this message in plain natural [LANGUAGE] for [AUDIENCE]. Keep it under 80 words, polite but direct, with one clear next action. Do not invent claims. Draft: [MESSAGE]

**08 — Meeting to action items**

> Extract decisions, pending questions, owners, deadlines and follow-ups from this meeting transcript [TRANSCRIPT]. Label unclear or missing details UNKNOWN rather than making them up.

**09 — Focused 45-minute work block**

> I have 45 minutes, energy level [LOW/MEDIUM/HIGH] and tasks [TASKS]. Rank the tasks by urgency and value. Produce a realistic schedule, including one simple way to measure progress.

**10 — SOP creator**

> Convert this repeated manual workflow [WORKFLOW] into a checklist for a new employee. Add prerequisites, safety checks, expected output, common failure cases, and how to stop safely if something goes wrong.

## 📚 Learning

**11 — Learn a topic**

> Teach [TOPIC] to someone who knows [CURRENT KNOWLEDGE] in 15 minutes. Give an analogy, three core ideas, one practical example, three common errors, and a 5-question knowledge check.

**12 — Study coach**

> Act as a tutor for [TOPIC]. Ask me one question at a time, check my answer before proceeding, and adapt difficulty to my actual progress. Do not reveal the next answer until I try.

**13 — Explain code**

> Explain what the following [LANGUAGE] code does, identify security and reliability risks, and provide two small tests. Do not execute changes or pretend they were tested. Code: [CODE]

**14 — Research verifier**

> Assess the claim [CLAIM]. Make a verification plan using primary sources, cite sources only if you can actually access them, and explicitly separate what is verified, contested, and unknown. Give me the fastest reliable check first.

**15 — Learning roadmap**

> Design a four-week learning plan for [SKILL] with [MINUTES PER DAY]. Include free resource categories, weekly small projects, objective tests, and a way to adapt if I fall behind.

## 🎬 Content & creativity

**16 — Short video concept**

> Give me five original 12–20 second video concepts for [AUDIENCE] who care about [TOPIC]. Each needs a visual hook in the first two seconds, authentic moving imagery, one clear takeaway, and a natural ending. No false urgency or exaggerated claims.

**17 — Caption that sounds human**

> Write three captions for this authentic video description [DESCRIPTION]. Voice: [TONE]. Use short natural language and only relevant hashtags. Keep the value of the video first and any promotional mention secondary.

**18 — Original visual brief**

> Create a concise visual direction brief for [IDEA]: subject, locations, shot list, camera movement, editing pace, lighting, textures, sound design and things to avoid. The result should feel like a real film rather than a slideshow.

**19 — Social content calendar**

> Make a seven-day content plan for [NICHE]. Mix practical tutorials, surprising facts with source verification, audience questions, comparisons and one recap. Provide one measurable hypothesis for each post. Avoid repetitive angles.

**20 — Improve an existing idea**

> Here is my creative idea [IDEA] and what I dislike [CRITICISM]. Identify the three most important weaknesses, propose one coherent replacement direction, and produce concrete shot-by-shot changes without adding decorative text.

## 💰 Personal decisions (not financial advice)

**21 — Choose without guessing prices**

> Compare [OPTIONS] for [GOAL] given a firm budget of [BUDGET]. Make a decision matrix with must-haves, trade-offs, risks and missing facts. Do not claim current prices or availability without verified sources.

**22 — Cash-flow explanation**

> Explain my monthly cash flow from these numbers [INCOME, BILLS, DEBTS] without assuming they are complete. Categorize fixed, variable and one-time items. Suggest ways to reduce waste and identify important missing details.

**23 — Negotiation script**

> Help me respectfully negotiate [PURCHASE OR SERVICE] at [ASKING PRICE] using only these true facts [FACTS]. Give me a concise first message and a polite response if the seller refuses. Do not invent competing bids.

**24 — First customer survey**

> Write five neutral questions to learn why people do or do not use [PRODUCT]. Avoid leading questions, separate usage from satisfaction, and tell me how to interpret responses from a very small sample without overclaiming.

**25 — Safer automation**

> Examine this repetitive task [TASK]. Separate read-only research, reversible actions, and irreversible actions. Propose a minimal automation with manual approval for risky steps, clear logging, a rollback path and a test plan. Do not claim any action has run.
